Apprenticeships in Wisconsin

Who runs it: Wisconsin Department of Workforce Development (DWD), Bureau of Apprenticeship Standards. A State Apprenticeship Agency registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. Choose an occupation and apply directly to the sponsor. Construction programs go through a local apprenticeship committee; other programs are run by employers.
  2. You must have a job opportunity with that sponsor, be at least 16, and generally have a high school diploma or equivalent.
  3. Meet the occupation's qualifications and enroll in the approved related instruction, usually at a technical college. The sponsor registers your contract.
  4. High school students: see Youth Apprenticeship. Not ready yet: see Certified Pre-Apprenticeship.
  5. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
