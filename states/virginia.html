Apprenticeships in Virginia

Who runs it: Virginia Works (the state workforce development agency) regulates and oversees Registered Apprenticeship. It does not place people in jobs.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

Eligibility and pay

  • Anyone 16 or older who is authorized to work and is hired or already employed by a registered sponsor in an apprenticeable occupation. Some occupations have restrictions for under-18s. There is no maximum age.
  • Pay is at least the state or federal minimum wage (whichever is higher), rising on a progressive scale set by the employer.
  • No cost to register. The sponsor may require tools or uniforms. The sponsor decides whether it pays for classroom training (RTI), shares the cost or has you pay. Workforce Development Centers may help with RTI costs.
  • When you finish you become a Journeyworker, with a national credential that is portable to all 50 states.

How to apply

  1. Search Virginia Works' list of active Registered Apprenticeship programs and apply directly to sponsors.
  2. Already working? Your employer can contact a Registered Apprenticeship Consultant to become a sponsor.
  3. Questions: 804-786-6030, apprenticeship@virginiaworks.gov.
