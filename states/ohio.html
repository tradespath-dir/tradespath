Apprenticeships in Ohio

Who runs it: The Ohio Department of Job and Family Services (ODJFS) is the State Apprenticeship Agency recognized by the U.S. Department of Labor. Its program brand is ApprenticeOhio, and its team registers apprenticeship programs.

How US apprenticeship works

Unlike in Canada, you don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

What to expect (per OhioMeansJobs)

  • At least 2,000 hours (about one year) of paid on-the-job training with an experienced worker.
  • At least 144 hours of classroom instruction for every 2,000 hours of on-the-job training.
  • A nationally recognized completion certificate. Programs span construction, manufacturing, health care, IT and more.

Eligibility

Set by each program sponsor.

How to find one

  1. Browse ApprenticeOhio's Statewide Registered Apprenticeship Dashboard for programs and sponsors in your area.
  2. Search for openings on apprenticeship.gov's Apprenticeship Finder.
  3. Apply directly to the employer or sponsor. For help, visit an OhioMeansJobs center.
