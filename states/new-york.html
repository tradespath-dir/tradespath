Apprenticeships in New York

Who runs it: New York State Department of Labor (NYSDOL), which approves programs and registers apprentice agreements. A State Apprenticeship Agency registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. You must be 18, or 16 to 17 with parental approval. Training lasts 1 to 6 years depending on the trade.
  2. Find openings on NYSDOL's Apprenticeship Announcements page or the NYS Job Bank, or contact your local apprenticeship office.
  3. Most sponsors require a high school diploma or equivalent. Some require specific courses, experience or physical ability.
  4. Not ready yet? A Direct Entry program (for example Helmets to Hardhats or Job Corps) builds skills and leads to an interview with a sponsor. Direct Entry providers can't charge tuition.
  5. Related instruction is usually free to the apprentice.
  6. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
