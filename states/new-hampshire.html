Apprenticeships in New Hampshire

Who runs it: ApprenticeshipNH (Community College System of NH) and NH Employment Security; programs are registered by the U.S. DOL Office of Apprenticeship. The U.S. Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. Find openings on ApprenticeshipNH's opportunities page or the Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder and apply to the employer.
  2. Typical requirements are age 16 or older, a high school diploma or GED, U.S. citizenship or permanent residency, and the ability to do the job.
  3. High school students can look at Registered Youth Apprenticeship. Pre-apprenticeship (for example WorkReadyNH) can build readiness.
  4. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
