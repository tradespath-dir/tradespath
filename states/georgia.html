Apprenticeships in Georgia

Who runs it: The U.S. DOL Office of Apprenticeship registers Georgia programs today. The Technical College System of Georgia (TCSG) is the state-designated apprenticeship agency and expects federal approval in Summer 2027. The U.S. Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. Find a registered program on the Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder, or ask one of Georgia's 22 technical colleges. Together they are the state's largest network of apprenticeship sponsors.
  2. Apply to the employer or sponsor and get hired as a paid employee.
  3. Typical programs require at least 2,000 hours of on-the-job training plus 144 hours of related classroom instruction for each 2,000 work hours. Most last 2 to 4 years.
  4. Through the workforce system, eligible apprentices may get help with tuition, books, tools and uniforms. Ask WorkSource Georgia.
  5. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
