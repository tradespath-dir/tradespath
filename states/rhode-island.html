Apprenticeships in Rhode Island

Who runs it: Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training (DLT). A State Apprenticeship Agency registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. Check DLT's Open Training Opportunities for programs that are recruiting. You apply to the employer or sponsor, not to DLT.
  2. Before applying, read the program's Standards and Apprenticeship Agreement for entry requirements, pay and length.
  3. You must be legally authorized to work in the U.S. Some licensed trades require age 18.
  4. Once you're accepted, your sponsor registers you. Licensed-trade apprentices need an apprenticeship card on job sites.
  5. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
