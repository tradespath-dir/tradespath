Apprenticeships in Washington State

Who runs it: The Apprenticeship Section of the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) registers apprentices accepted into approved programs.

How US apprenticeship works

Unlike in Canada, you don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

Eligibility

  • In general, you must be capable of doing the work (with or without reasonable accommodations), have the skills and abilities to learn the trade, and provide proof of age, a high school diploma or equivalent, and other required documents.
  • Costs depend on the program. Both union and non-union programs exist, and joining a union is your choice.
  • You can ask for credit for prior experience, education or military service. The program's committee decides.

How to apply

  1. Search L&I's Apprenticeship Registration & Tracking System (ARTS) by county and occupation, and read each program's standards.
  2. Apply directly to the program (many have online applications). Expect an interview and possibly an aptitude test.
  3. If you're accepted, the sponsor registers you with L&I.
  4. Not ready yet? Look at a Recognized Apprenticeship Preparation program.

Note for electricians: trainees doing (01) general electrician work must be registered in an approved Washington, Oregon or Montana apprenticeship program.
