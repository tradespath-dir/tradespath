Apprenticeships in Nevada

Who runs it: Nevada State Apprenticeship Council, Office of the Labor Commissioner. A State Apprenticeship Agency registers programs here.

How US apprenticeship works

You don't register with the government yourself. You apply to an employer or program sponsor, and the sponsor registers you once you're accepted. Registered apprentices are paid employees who get on-the-job training plus classroom instruction and earn a nationally recognized credential.

How to start

  1. Find programs and contacts in the Office of the Labor Commissioner's registered apprenticeship program list.
  2. Apply directly to a program that is accepting applicants. The Council does not take individual applications.
  3. You generally must be 18 and have a high school diploma or GED (or get one within a year), and meet physical and screening requirements. Pass any aptitude test. Programs rank applicants and fill openings as they come up.
  4. Search openings anywhere in the US: Apprenticeship.gov Apprenticeship Finder.

Money help

Some apprentices taking college-credit related instruction may qualify for federal aid: see Paying for trade school (Pell Grants). Ask your local American Job Center about WIOA training funds.
