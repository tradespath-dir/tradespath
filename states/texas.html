Apprenticeships in Texas

Who runs it: Registered Apprenticeship programs are approved and registered by the U.S. Department of Labor's Office of Apprenticeship. The Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) supports them through ApprenticeshipTexas. Employers or sponsors run the actual programs.

How it differs from Canada

There is no single government office where you register yourself. You find an employer or program sponsor with a registered program and apply directly to them.

What a registered apprenticeship includes (per TWC)

  • You are a paid, full-time employee (W-2) from day one, with raises as your skills grow.
  • Structured on-the-job learning with a mentor or journey-level worker (TWC lists at least 2,000 hours per year) plus related technical instruction (at least 144 hours per year).
  • On completion, a nationally recognized certificate issued by the U.S. Department of Labor.

Eligibility

Set by each program. Generally at least 16 (for hazardous occupations), with a high school diploma or GED unless the program says otherwise, and able to meet the physical demands of the job.

How to find one

  1. Search the federal Apprenticeship Finder on apprenticeship.gov or TWC's apprenticeship job search.
  2. Apply directly to the employer or sponsor.
  3. Need help? Visit an American Job Center / local Workforce Solutions office.
