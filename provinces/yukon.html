Apprenticeships in Yukon

Who runs it: Apprenticeship and Trade Certification, Government of Yukon (Department of Education). You can register or certify in 40 designated trades under the Apprentice Training Act.

How it works

  • About 80% of your time is paid on-the-job learning with a certified journeyperson, and 20% is in-school technical training (4 to 10 weeks a year, depending on the trade). It usually takes 2 to 4 years.
  • In-school training can be at Yukon University, in Alberta (under a training agreement), or in other provinces, but your employer and residency must be in Yukon.
  • Each trade has its own math entrance requirement (Grade 10, 11 or 12); see the designated trades sheet.

Eligibility

  • At least 16, a Yukon resident, and meet your trade's entrance requirements.
  • Work for an employer who employs a journeyperson certified in your trade and will sponsor you, pay the legal wage, and release you for technical training each year.

How to apply

  1. Complete the Apprenticeship application and agreement form; your employer completes their section.
  2. Attach transcripts, any trade training certificates, and your supervising journeyperson's certificate. Previous experience may count (300+ hours needs a Trade Experience Verification form).
  3. Pay the $10 non-refundable fee and submit. Processing takes 4 to 6 weeks, then you complete an orientation to get your record book and wallet card.

For high school students: STEP into the Trades

A pilot that pays high school students to gain trade work experience before registering as apprentices, which can also count as high school credits. For summer 2026 the trades were construction electrician, carpentry, heavy equipment technician and welding; you needed to be 16+ by June 10, 2026, a Yukon resident, and a full-time Yukon high school student for at least one semester. Ask your shop teacher or guidance counsellor; check for future intakes.
