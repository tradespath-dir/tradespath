Apprenticeships in Atlantic Canada

Each Atlantic province runs its own system. In all four, you generally need a job with an employer who has a certified journeyperson to supervise you.

New Brunswick

  • Who runs it: Skilled Trades New Brunswick (STNB).
  • Apply online through the Apprenticeship Management System (AMS) self-serve portal, which apprentices, employers and youth pre-apprentices (high school and college students) use to register, book courses and exams, and track hours.
  • Your local Skilled Trades NB office can explain options first.

Nova Scotia

  • Who runs it: Nova Scotia Apprenticeship Agency (NSAA). Since January 8, 2026, apprentices use its online AMS to register and manage training.
  • You and your employer complete an apprenticeship agreement, and you must be supervised by a certified journeyperson. A registration/ID card fee applies.
  • Youth Apprentice: aged 16 to 19, working toward a high school diploma and employed full- or part-time in a designated trade. Youth apprentices pay only the ID card cost (under $20).
  • In compulsory trades, ask an Industry Training Consultant about a temporary permit while registration is processed.

Prince Edward Island

  • Who runs it: Government of PEI (SkillsPEI apprenticeship).
  • Youth Apprenticeship Program (ASAP): age 16+, enrolled in school, and working for a wage in a designated trade. Find the job, submit an application, then get a skills logbook.
  • Logged hours transfer into post-secondary apprenticeship. High school trade courses with 70%+ can transfer up to 110 hours each (if board-approved).
  • With 250+ logged paid hours you can apply in your graduating year for a $1,000 award ($500 bursary + $500 Holland College tuition credit).

Newfoundland and Labrador

  • Who runs it: Apprenticeship and Trades Certification Division (ATCD).
  • Two pathways: entry-level (pre-employment) training at a post-secondary institution (often about 9 months; you are not yet an apprentice but may get Level 1 credit), or Direct Entry with no prior training.
  • To register: get a job with a certified journeyperson, submit the application with a signed MOU (or LOU for work outside NL) and your official transcript, then receive a registration card and logbook.
  • Registered apprentices receive free tuition and books for in-school training, and may qualify for EI during training.
  • Youth Apprenticeship Summer Program (YASP): 7 weeks of paid summer work for Grade 10 or 11 students (parent permission and teacher recommendation needed). Hours count as official apprenticeship hours.
