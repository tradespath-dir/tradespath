Apprenticeships in New Brunswick

Who runs it: Skilled Trades NB (Government of New Brunswick).

Key facts

  • 44 occupations have apprenticeship programs; 12 are compulsory occupations.
  • Programs take 2 to 4 years: about 80% paid on-the-job training and 20% technical (in-school) training.
  • Your employer pays you, usually more as you complete each stage. You train under a qualified journeyperson.
  • Finishing earns a Diploma of Apprenticeship and a Certificate of Qualification. Red Seal trades add an interprovincial endorsement.

How to become an apprentice

  1. Get hired in a designated trade.
  2. Register through the Apprenticeship Management System (AMS) self-serve portal. You can create an account, submit an apprenticeship application, sign up for courses and exams, and log your hours.
  3. Check the board orders for registration prerequisites and the hours each trade requires.
  4. Ask for credit for high school pre-apprenticeship (including the NB Teen Apprenticeship Program), trade-related summer work, college pre-employment programs and verifiable work experience.
  5. Experienced workers can instead challenge the certification exam through Trade Qualification.

Money help

Eligible apprentices may get EI during technical training. They may also qualify for the Training and Skills Development (TSD) program, which can cover training, child care, living away from home, travel and commuting costs. Apprenticeship incentives are also available. See the federal apprentice supports page too.
