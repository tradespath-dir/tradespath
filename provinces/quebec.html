Apprenticeships in Quebec

Quebec works differently from the rest of Canada. There are two separate systems, depending on the kind of trade.

1. Construction trades: Commission de la construction du Québec (CCQ)

  • To work in construction covered by Quebec's construction labour law (Loi R-20), you need a competency certificate issued by the CCQ. Apprentices hold a certificat de compétence apprenti (CCA), which states the trade you can work in.
  • Routes to a CCA include graduating from a recognized program for the trade (usually a DEP, the vocational diploma), entry when the regional labour pool is short, recognized experience, or being hired as a student, among others.
  • Apprenticeship is counted in periods of 2,000 hours. Examples: carpenter-joiner, painter and bricklayer-mason take 3 periods (6,000 h); electrician and pipefitter take 4 (8,000 h); elevator mechanic takes 5 (10,000 h).
  • Your carnet d'apprentissage (apprenticeship booklet) tracks hours, training credits and when you can sit the provincial qualification exam.
  • In 2026 the CCQ is running paid work-study pathways (alternance travail-études) toward a DEP in priority trades (bricklaying, carpentry, electricity, plumbing/heating, refrigeration) for people who already hold a CCQ certificate.

2. Other trades: Workplace Apprenticeship Program (PAMT)

  • Run by the Government of Quebec through Services Québec (the network formerly known as Emploi-Québec).
  • A less-experienced employee is paired with an experienced compagnon at the same company while being paid.
  • It lasts 3 to 36 months depending on the trade and your experience, with at least 21 hours a week on apprenticeship tasks.
  • It can lead to a certificate of qualification or an attestation of competence. A Services Québec qualification agent supports you for free.
  • You must already be employed; check that your trade is on the PAMT list.

Language: most official information and training is in French.
